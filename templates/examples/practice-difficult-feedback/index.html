Giving hard feedback with SBI

The big idea

Describe what happened, not what it means: a specific moment, a visible behavior, and its real effect.

You will be able to: open a hard conversation calmly, say a Situation-Behavior-Impact sentence the other person cannot dispute, and keep going when they get defensive.

The situation

You are Maya, lead of a small product team. On Thursday your developer Dev, who is strong technically, answered a client's pricing question with "that's not my area" and looked at his laptop. You want to talk to him, and you know he will expect bad news.

The conversation, step by step

Press the step buttons. The meter is Dev's tension. It is a teaching model with made-up numbers: the rules behind it (verdicts raise it; specifics and curiosity lower it) are the claim, not the percentages.

What each part has to pass

PartTest before you say itMaya's version
SituationCould Dev find the moment on a calendar?"Thursday, in the Q&A at the end of the client demo"
BehaviorWould a video camera have recorded it?"You said 'that's not my area' and looked at your laptop"
ImpactIs it what happened to me, the team or the work?"I felt caught off guard; Priya asked who owns pricing"
Intent (ask)Am I asking before I conclude why?"What was going on for you in that moment?"

Your turn as the lead

A different case. Sam, on your team, joined the 9:15 standup at 9:25 on Monday, Tuesday and Thursday this week. You are about to talk to Sam.

You said the Behavior line and Sam nodded. Now the Impact.

Sam answers quickly and a little sharply.

Keep it

Try it this week
  • Today: write one SBI sentence about something small a colleague did, positive or critical, and say it to them.
  • Before your next hard talk: write S, B and I on paper, then cross out every word a camera could not have recorded.
  • In the talk: after the Impact, ask one open question about intent and let the person finish before you reply.
